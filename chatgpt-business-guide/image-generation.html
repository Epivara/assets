Image Generation - STUB

ChatGPT can generate images for slides, mockups, and marketing.

What's actually happening

When you ask for a picture, ChatGPT doesn't draw it itself. It hands your request off to a separate AI model built for one job: turning a text description into an image. That model learned by studying an enormous pile of images from the internet, so it's very good at producing something that looks right — but it's matching patterns it has seen before, not understanding what it's making.

This is why it struggles with anything specific to us. The image model has never seen Epivara. It has no idea what our logo looks like or how our name is spelled, so if you ask for either, it will confidently invent something close-but-wrong.

Handing it our real logo doesn't fix this the way you'd hope. ChatGPT doesn't “see” the picture and hold onto it — it writes itself a short text description of what's in it (something like “a navy geometric shape next to the word Epivara”) and passes those words to the image model. The image model then paints its best guess from that description. Whatever the words left out is simply gone, which is why the logo never comes back exactly right.

The short version: there's no real visual understanding here. It's a very capable guesser working from a text description, not a designer who knows our brand. Once you expect that, it behaves predictably — and it's exactly why the workflow below works.

Generate clean, then brand Image generation makes up logos and company names and garbles exact text. The reliable workflow is: generate the imagery with no branding, then add your real logo and text afterward (by hand, or in whatever design tool you use). Don't ship AI-invented logos.

Try it yourself

Generate a background image with no text or logos.

Ask ChatGPT:

“Make a clean abstract background for a title slide, no text.”

Expected outcome: You get artwork you can brand yourself afterward.
