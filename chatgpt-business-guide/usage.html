Usage

ChatGPT is powerful, but it isn't all unlimited — some of it is metered. This page explains, in plain terms, how that works so nothing catches you off guard.

What's a token?

Everything you type, and everything ChatGPT writes back, is measured in tokens — small chunks of text, a little shorter than a word on average. When people talk about "usage," they really mean how many tokens are going back and forth. A quick question costs very few; a long document, a big attachment, or a task that runs for a while costs a lot more.

The part that matters: Chat vs. Work & Codex

Not everything is metered the same way:

  • Chat — the everyday chatting this guide starts with — is unlimited on our plan. Use it as much as you like.
  • Work and Codex share a separate weekly limit. Our plan includes a set amount each week; once it's used up, you wait for it to reset.

Why the difference? Work and Codex don't just reply — they go off and do things (reading, running steps, working through a longer task), which uses far more behind the scenes. That's why they're metered when plain Chat isn't.

Rule of thumb If you're just chatting, you don't need to think about usage at all. Only Work and Codex draw down the weekly limit, so that's the only place it's worth being a little deliberate.

It adds up faster than you'd expect

It's worth knowing how quickly heavy use can add up. A single involved Work or Codex session — say, grinding through a long document over and over — can get through a surprising amount in a short time. You won't run up a bill by accident, but the weekly limit is real, so pacing yourself on big tasks keeps you from hitting the wall in the middle of a project.
