Desktop App

Everything in this guide works in your browser, so you never need the desktop app. But if you'd rather have ChatGPT in its own window — always a click away, no browser tab to lose — there's an app for Mac and Windows.

A little heads-up The desktop app grew out of a developer tool (Codex), and you can still feel those roots. It's being smoothed out for everyday use, but it has a bit more of a learning curve than the website — a few different modes, and some things billed separately. If you just want to get going, start in the browser and come back to this later.

Chat vs. Work vs. Codex

The app has more than one mode, and they don't all behave like the website:

  • Chat here is basically the same chat as the web, with one nicety: a companion window you can pop open over whatever you're working on with a keyboard shortcut, ask something, and dismiss. One catch — the agents you build in the web workspace don't come along; they only work on the web.
  • Work and Codex are the real reason to install the app. These are the modes that can reach your local files and grind through longer, multi-step jobs. They also draw down the separate weekly limit — see Usage.

Is it worth it for you?

For most people, honestly, no. If you're chatting, drafting, and using plugins, the web is the better home — it has your workspace agents and is easier to get around. The desktop app earns its place when you have serious, long-running work that needs your local files, which is Work and Codex territory. If that's not you, you're not missing anything by staying in the browser.

One genuinely desktop-only trick Paired with Voice, the desktop app (especially on Mac) can see your on-screen window for context and help you through what you're doing live. Handy in the moment, but it's a Voice / Work feature rather than something plain chat needs — and these desktop capabilities move quickly, so confirm before leaning on any specific one.
