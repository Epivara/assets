Cognitive Offloading - STUB

When you let a tool do your thinking for you, it's called cognitive offloading. It's not new — a calculator, a GPS, and spell-check are all things we hand a mental task to. What is new is how much you can hand off at once: with Work or Codex you can offload an entire job, start to finish, without doing any of the reasoning in the middle.

That's a genuinely good deal most of the time. The catch is that skills stay sharp by being used. Delegate the thinking often enough and the underlying skill quietly fades — the same way most people can no longer navigate their own city without GPS. Offload everything, without planning or checking the work, and over time you get slower and less confident at the very tasks you used to own.

Whether that actually matters comes down to one bet: will the tool always be good enough to not need you? If it is, the atrophy is harmless — you never have to step in. But the day it gets something wrong, hits a problem it can't handle, or simply isn't available, you need the judgment to catch it and take over. And catching a confident, wrong-looking result (see Work) takes exactly the expertise that offloading erodes. Nobody knows yet whether the tools will stay reliably ahead of us, so betting your whole skillset on it is a gamble.

There's no clean rule here, just a posture: offload the grunt work, keep the judgment. Stay close enough to the important calls that you'd notice if the tool got one wrong. Use it to move faster — not to stop thinking.
